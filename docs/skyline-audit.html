What works, and what still belongs

Seventy features read against one question: does SkyLine still need this? Thirty-six are vestigial — leftovers from the flight tracker this app used to be. Cutting them removes about 17,000 of 39,230 lines and loses nothing the place log does.

Wire OnboardingState.requestPresentation to a “Find my places” button in the place log’s empty state, replacing “Add a trip”.

The whole-library scan runs exactly once per install. Its re-entry point is built, documented, and has zero call sites — so the only way to see the core loop twice is to reinstall, which then dies at the sign-in wall. About twenty lines, and it cannot regress anything, because nothing calls it today. It turns every other item here from reproducible-once into reproducible-in-ten-seconds.

Five bugs, one line

The deck asks a Trip where a place is. Photo-detected places have no real trip — LibraryEpisode.asTrip() fabricates one carrying only an id, title and dates, and it is never saved. So candidatePlace reads country, timezone, city and coordinate off a container that has none.

Consequences: every place files under one “Unknown” country, the summary card reads 0 countries, no flags render, dates show in the reader’s timezone, and row subtitles read “4 Mar 2026 – 9 Mar 2026” where a city should be. CountryLocator already does this lookup offline and is already preloaded — it is just wired to the wrong caller.

Demo data is in your real iCloud

FlightStore.loadFlights() seeds four fabricated flights when no saved flights exist, and PlaceImportService.importFlights turns every endpoint into a permanently unrated Place — then syncs it to the private database.

On a fresh install, before judging anything, the log contains eight airports the user has never been to. They pad every country section, inflate the “still undecided” count, and appear as grey dots on the globe. Both detection services deliberately suppress airports; this path overrides that. It is a four-line deletion.

The globe outranks the log

isGlobeReady is both the splash flag and the isPresented binding for the sheet the entire product lives in. The log cannot appear until the globe says it is ready — and the globe cannot show anything about the log: PLACE_SELECTED is three lines of print, and focusOnPlace has no Swift callers.

Cost: a WebKit process, globe.gl from a CDN with no bundled fallback, an 820 KB geojson inlined into an HTML string, a three-second launch gate, and roughly 1,800 lines. Benefit: sixteen name pills and dots you cannot tap.
